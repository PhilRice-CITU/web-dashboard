# Quality grades

Every graded scan receives one overall **quality grade** derived from the
defects found in the sample.

## The grade scale

The system assigns one of seven possible outcomes, from best to worst:

| Grade | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Premium** | Highest quality — all defect factors are within the strictest limits |
| **Grade no. 1** | Very good quality |
| **Grade no. 2** | Good quality |
| **Grade no. 3** | Acceptable quality |
| **Grade no. 4** | Below standard quality |
| **Grade no. 5** | Lowest passing quality |
| **Off-Grade** | Sample exceeded the limits of every defined grade |

**Off-Grade** does not mean the measurement failed — it means the sample's
defect levels were too high to qualify for even Grade no. 5. It is a valid
outcome that indicates the sample falls outside the PNS/BAFS 290:2025
classification range.

## How the grade is decided

The pipeline measures each of the five graded defect factors as a percentage of
the sample, then compares those percentages to the PNS/BAFS 290:2025 thresholds.
The overall grade is determined by the **worst-performing factor** — a single
out-of-threshold defect can lower the overall grade.

## Reading a grade

On a result, the quality grade is shown alongside the metrics that produced it,
so you can see which defect drove the outcome. A lower grade is not an error —
it is an accurate measurement of the sample.

  Confirm the exact grade names and thresholds against your copy of PNS/BAFS
  290:2025 before using this page operationally.
</Callout>
